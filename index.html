What this means

The second purchase is decided in the first eight weeks. After that the customer is effectively gone, and no amount of month-4 win-back pulls them back at scale.

So the money is not in a lapsed-customer program. It is in a category-based cross-sell journey that fires inside day 0–60, while the customer is still warm and still remembers the brand.

Best cohorts confirm it: Apr 2026 hit 15.3% at month 0, Mar 2026 14.33%. The demand is there. Nothing is catching it.

Biggest unlocks · India

Build order

  1. 01

    Lead capture with a first-order incentive

    No popup, no list growth, no owned audience. Everything downstream depends on this running first. A first-order discount on a segmented popup is the single highest-leverage change on the site.

  2. 02

    Abandoned browse, cart and checkout

    Pure hygiene, and the fastest revenue in the build. 17,921 people showed intent and left with no follow-up. Checkout abandonment first, then cart, then browse.

  3. 03

    Category-based cross-sell inside 60 days

    Triggered off first category purchased, sequenced across day 7 to day 55. This is the flow that attacks the month-2 cliff directly, and it is where India's repeat behaviour actually pays.

  4. 04

    Four channels, one journey

    WhatsApp carries the urgency, email carries the merchandising, SMS carries the deadline, RCS upgrades the experience where handsets support it. One decision tree, not four separate programs.

A younger market

Read

The US is newer and repeat rates are thinner month over month. There is not enough repeat history yet to build a cross-sell program off observed behaviour alone, which changes the order of operations rather than the destination.

The consequence: list building is not step one because it is tidy. It is step one because without an owned audience there is nothing to retain, and paid acquisition carries the entire number.

Difference that matters

India can lead with cross-sell because repeat intent already exists inside 60 days. The US has to manufacture the second purchase with a deliberate post-purchase program before cross-sell has anything to work with.

Direct mail replaces WhatsApp as the high-attention channel. It is slower and costlier per touch, so it is reserved for first-time buyers and high-value segments, not the whole file.

Biggest unlocks · US

Build order

  1. 01

    Lead capture, weighted even heavier than India

    With fewer organic repeats, the owned list is the only compounding asset in this market. Every month without a popup is a month of paid traffic bought once and never re-reachable.

  2. 02

    Abandoned browse, cart and checkout

    Same hygiene layer, same sequence. Fastest path to proving the channel pays before larger spend is committed.

  3. 03

    Post-purchase flows

    The US-specific unlock. Order confirmation, shipping, education, review request and a timed second-order nudge. This is what creates the repeat behaviour India already has.

  4. 04

    Category-based cross-sell inside 60 days

    Layered once post-purchase has produced enough second orders to segment against. Same 0–60 day window, built on a base that exists rather than one assumed.

  5. 05

    Email, SMS, direct mail

    No WhatsApp in this market. Direct mail takes the high-attention slot for first-time buyers and top-decile customers.

Assumptions and sources
  • AOV of $95, supplied by the brand.
  • Funnel window is 492,526 sessions. At 130,000 sessions per 30 days, that window is 3.79 months, which is how window figures convert to monthly.
  • Abandoners with intent = added to cart (20,536) minus completed checkout (2,615). Includes both cart and checkout abandonment; browse abandonment sits above this and is upside, not counted.
  • Welcome flow: 130,000 sessions in the last 30 days, of which 100,000 assumed new visitors, of which 50% stay long enough to see the popup.
  • Welcome placed-order rate held at a conservative 3%. Published benchmark median across ecommerce brands is 4.37%, with a wide spread.
  • Flows only. Campaign revenue is excluded from every figure on this page.
Delivery · first six months

Nothing here is exotic. It is sequencing.

The order matters because each phase feeds the next: capture builds the audience, hygiene proves the channel, cross-sell compounds it.

Phase 1 · weeks 1–4

Capture and hygiene

  • Popup with first-order incentive, both markets
  • Welcome series
  • Abandoned checkout, then cart
  • Deliverability and sending infrastructure
Phase 2 · weeks 5–12

Depth

  • Browse abandonment
  • Post-purchase series, US-weighted
  • WhatsApp and SMS layered into India journeys
  • Campaign calendar goes live
Phase 3 · months 4–6

Compounding

  • Category cross-sell, day 0–60
  • RCS pilot in India
  • Direct mail to US high-value segments
  • Segmentation and win-back
Six-month target

Flows alone carry the model above. With a campaign calendar running on top of a list that grows by roughly 2,500 contacts a month, 25–30% of total revenue coming from Klaviyo within six months is a realistic target, not a stretch one.
