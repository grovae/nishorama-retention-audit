Two markets. Two different problems. Zero flows running in either.

Nishorama is buying traffic in India and the US and has no lifecycle program in either market. Every abandoned cart, every first-time buyer, every reachable profile sits unworked. This audit reads each market on its own data, then sets one build sequence across both.

Start · 2 per week → Ramp · 3–4 per week

Open at two sends a week while deliverability and sending reputation establish, then move to three or four. A list this size supports that cadence comfortably provided sends are segmented rather than blasted to the full file.

What this means

The second purchase is decided in the first eight weeks. After that the customer is effectively gone, and no amount of month-4 win-back pulls them back at scale.

So the money is not in a lapsed-customer program. It is in a category-based cross-sell journey that fires inside day 0–60, while the customer is still warm and still remembers the brand.

Best cohorts confirm it: Apr 2026 hit 15.3% at month 0, Mar 2026 14.33%. The demand is there. Nothing is catching it.

The approach

Cross-sell built on purchase data, not on merchandising instinct. Every first order tells you what the customer is dressing for. A Short Kurti buyer is one Relaxed Pants away from a complete outfit, and one step away from buying Sets instead — the highest-value item in the catalogue.

The build: map first category to next category across every repeat customer, rank the paths by volume and value, then automate the top four as triggered journeys inside the 0–60 day window. The map below shows what that analysis looks like once the data is connected.

What it exposes

Read 1

Seven in ten repeats are the same category bought again, with nothing prompting it. That is unmanaged demand, and the cheapest revenue in the business to capture with a replenishment nudge.

The paths to automate

Read 2

Short Kurti → Sets, Short Kurti → Relaxed Pants, Relaxed Pants → Top, Sets → Short Kurti. Four journeys covering the bulk of all cross-category movement, each triggered off first category purchased.

Why it pays

Read 3

The Short Kurti → Sets path moves a customer from a ₹1,267 item to a ₹2,105 one. Same customer, same acquisition cost, 66% more order value. That is the case for building this on data rather than guesswork.

Biggest unlocks · India

Build order

  1. 01

    Lead capture with a first-order incentive

    No popup, no list growth, no owned audience beyond what already exists. A first-order discount on a segmented popup is the single highest-leverage change on the site.

  2. 02

    Abandoned browse, cart and checkout

    Pure hygiene, and the fastest revenue in the build. Checkout abandonment first, then cart, then browse.

  3. 03

    Category-based cross-sell inside 60 days

    Triggered off first category purchased, sequenced across day 7 to day 55. This is the flow that attacks the month-2 cliff directly, and where India's repeat behaviour actually pays.

  4. 04

    Four channels, one journey

    WhatsApp carries the urgency, email carries the merchandising, SMS carries the deadline, RCS upgrades the experience where handsets support it. One decision tree, not four separate programs.

List status · US

A small list, and two thirds of it has never bought.

2,724 Reachable customers Have bought at least once.
8,565 Reachable profiles One eighteenth the size of the India list.
~5,841 Profiles yet to purchase Roughly 68% of the list. More than double India's share.

Campaign cadence

Sending plan · US
Minimum · 2 per week → Hold until list growth supports more

Two sends a week from day one. The list is too small to ramp harder without fatiguing it, and too small to split into many tracks, so the calendar stays simple until lead capture starts adding volume.

With 68% of the file yet to place an order, the US campaign calendar is a prospect conversion program first and a retention program second. That is the inverse of India, and it is why the popup matters more here than anywhere else in the account.

A younger market

Read

Repeat rates are thinner month over month. There is not enough repeat history yet to build a cross-sell program off observed behaviour alone, which changes the order of operations rather than the destination.

The consequence: list building is not step one because it is tidy. It is step one because without an owned audience there is nothing to retain, and paid acquisition carries the entire number.

Difference that matters

India can lead with cross-sell because repeat intent already exists inside 60 days. The US has to manufacture the second purchase with a deliberate post-purchase program before cross-sell has anything to work with.

Direct mail replaces WhatsApp as the high-attention channel. Slower and costlier per touch, so it is reserved for first-time buyers and high-value segments, not the whole file.

Biggest unlocks · US

Build order

  1. 01

    Lead capture, weighted even heavier than India

    With fewer organic repeats, the owned list is the only compounding asset in this market. Every month without a popup is a month of paid traffic bought once and never re-reachable.

  2. 02

    Abandoned browse, cart and checkout

    17,921 people showed intent and left with no follow-up. Fastest path to proving the channel pays before larger spend is committed.

  3. 03

    Post-purchase flows

    The US-specific unlock. Order confirmation, shipping, education, review request and a timed second-order nudge. This is what creates the repeat behaviour India already has.

  4. 04

    Category-based cross-sell inside 60 days

    Layered once post-purchase has produced enough second orders to segment against. Same 0–60 day window, built on a base that exists rather than one assumed.

  5. 05

    Email, SMS, direct mail

    No WhatsApp in this market. Direct mail takes the high-attention slot for first-time buyers and top-decile customers.

Assumptions and sources
  • Every figure in this section is US only. No India data is blended in.
  • AOV of $95, supplied by the brand for the US market.
  • US funnel window is 492,526 sessions. At 130,000 sessions per 30 days, that window is 3.79 months, which is how window figures convert to monthly.
  • Abandoners with intent = added to cart (20,536) minus completed checkout (2,615). Includes cart and checkout abandonment; browse abandonment sits above this and is upside, not counted.
  • Welcome flow: 130,000 US sessions in the last 30 days, of which 100,000 assumed new visitors, of which 50% stay long enough to see the popup.
  • Welcome placed-order rate held at a conservative 3%. Published benchmark median across ecommerce brands is 4.37%, with a wide spread.
  • Flows only. Campaign revenue is excluded from every figure in this section.
  • Profiles yet to purchase, in both market panels, is derived: reachable profiles minus customers. It assumes the customer count sits inside the reachable pool, so treat it as an approximate size, not an exact segment.
  • The India cross-sell map uses illustrative sample data on Nishorama's real categories, to show the shape of the analysis. It is replaced with actual purchase-path data once Shopify is connected.

Nothing here is exotic. It is sequencing.

One programme run across both stores, with the market-specific pieces marked. Each phase feeds the next: capture builds the audience, hygiene proves the channel, cross-sell compounds it.
